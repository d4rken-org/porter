<i>Porter</i> gives your apps ADB access, so they can do things that normally need a computer. You decide which apps get it.

With ADB access, apps can manage other apps, clear hidden caches or reach files Android normally keeps locked. Usually that means connecting a computer and typing commands. Porter starts a small service once, and the apps you approve use it directly on your device.

Porter is a maintained fork of Shizuku, kept small and stable for the apps that rely on it.

<b>Start it your way</b>
- Wireless debugging on Android 11 or newer, no computer needed
- USB debugging with a computer, on Android 7.0 or newer
- Root, if your device is already rooted
- Start on boot, and a watchdog that restarts Porter if it crashes (Android 11+ or root)
- Android TV support, with pairing you can finish using the remote

<b>Stay in control</b>
- Apps have to ask first, and you approve or deny each one
- See which installed apps support Porter and which are connected
- Revoke access at any time, or pause it for all apps at once
- Control Porter from automation apps using intents

<b>Apps built for Shizuku</b>
Apps with direct Porter support only need Porter. Apps that only support Shizuku need an extra step, explained at https://porter.darken.eu/compatibility

<b>For app developers</b>
Add direct Porter support to your app: https://porter.darken.eu/developers

<b>Privacy</b>
No ads, no tracking, no account. Only apps you approve get access. Porter is open source.

<b>Accessibility service</b>
Porter includes an optional accessibility service that is only used on Android TV to pair wireless debugging. It reads the pairing code from Android's pairing screen and turns itself off once pairing is done. It does not collect or share any other information.

Porter builds on Shizuku by RikkaApps and later maintenance work by thedjchi and contributors. It is not affiliated with the Shizuku developers.
